NeoHorse-1: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement via Agentic Post-Training with Routing Harness. Lead authors Guoliang Cao, Guohao Dai, and Tianyu Guo, from a large alphabetical roster at TokenRhythm Technologies, Infinigence AI, Tsinghua University, Peking University, and The Chinese University of Hong Kong. This is a technical report for a family of agent-native models, and the framing question is recursive self-improvement: can a system observe its own capabilities and turn that evidence into the next round of training?

The authors' claim is that you do not need exotic machinery to start this loop. A deployed routing harness - the execution layer that decides which model serves each request - already records three things on every turn: an execution trajectory, an estimate of capability demand, and an observable outcome. Their system couples a heterogeneous model pool with intelligent routing, converts those records into user-turn training examples, and feeds evaluation results back into the next training mixture.

Here's how the data side works, because that's where most of the engineering lives. The corpus is on the order of 100000 to 1000000 harness-generated trajectories per the paper, a deliberately wide band. Each record goes through structural validation: the pipeline reconstructs requests, responses, tool calls, and observations, then checks payload readability, causal event order, and closure of tool-call result pairs. Trajectories are graded as complete, partially recoverable, or quarantined - missing responses and orphan tool observations get caught here. Survivors then face semantic evaluation on six dimensions: goal attainment, instruction adherence, tool use, evidence consistency, error recovery, and termination, each marked PASS, WARN, FAIL, or not evaluated. Missing evidence is never converted into a positive verdict, which is the right default. Structured facts established by deterministic rules cannot be overwritten by the semantic judge, and every attribute keeps its derivation method and confidence.
Characterization happens at the subscene level - adjacent user turns sharing a local goal - along three axes: Scene, Goal, and Outcome. Scene covers task type and domain, Goal decomposes the user objective into acceptance criteria with cross-turn relations like new, continued, modified, or resumed, and Outcome records the verifiable result against that goal. Alongside this, the harness router assigns each turn one of four service tiers: C0 for bounded low-risk requests, C1 as the general-purpose default, C2 for multi-step reasoning and execution, and C3 for maximum capability. For each turn the corpus keeps the router's raw prediction, the policy-adjusted decision, and the tier actually served, so predicted demand, policy constraints, and executed action stay independently analyzable.

The training method has two parts. First, routing-guided curriculum SFT. Because the served model reflects user overrides and availability, they re-estimate capability demand from the request and history, giving each example a soft routing score - the score-weighted mean tier index, between zero and three. Training proceeds over three roughly equal stages, shifting the mix toward higher-scored examples while reserving some low-scored examples for the last stage so training doesn't end dominated by demanding interactions. Same masked SFT objective throughout; no optimizer reset or learning-rate restart between stages.

Second, routing-guided on-policy distillation. Student rollouts start from recorded contexts, again scheduled by routing score over three stages. A fixed teacher supplies next-token distributions on the student's generated prefixes, and the loss is a response-normalized reverse KL. For compact supervision they keep the student's top K candidate tokens at each position and put all remaining probability mass into one extra bin, so both distributions are coarsened over K plus one bins. Only the student updates; the rollout checkpoint refreshes as training proceeds.
Now the numbers. Across ten benchmarks spanning harness-based agents, tool use, coding, and instruction following, post-training lifts the macro-average from 58.94 to 64.87 for the 4B model and from 65.60 to 69.04 for the 9B model. At 4B, NeoHorse-1-4B beats Qwen3.5-4B on every benchmark where both have results, averaging 64.87 against 58.94 and landing ahead of Spark-X2.5-4B at 62.22, Agents-A1-4B at 61.46, and Nanbeige-4.2-3B at 62.31. Gains concentrate on harness-based tasks like QwenClawBench and PinchBench. Evaluation is detailed: SGLang serving, three runs for several benchmarks, and a candid note that VitaBench's recommended user-simulator model was no longer available, so they substituted DeepSeek-V4-Flash as both simulator and judge.

There's a controlled data-source comparison: starting from the same Qwen3.5-4B checkpoint with the same curriculum, seed, and budget, routing-harness trajectories beat the public Toucan tool-agent dataset on all five comparable benchmarks, improving the unweighted average by 6.26 points, with the largest gains of 8.54 and 11.31 points on HumanEval and tau2-Bench. Scaling supervision from about 2 million to 10 million unique tokens moves a five-benchmark average from 69.31 to 71.45 - real but flattening.

The feedback loop itself is only lightly validated. Capability-guided allocation shifts the next mixture toward underperforming regions, but the report covers a single pass of the evaluation-selection-update loop. The authors say this plainly: whether gains accumulate across iterations remains to be tested, and evaluation concentrates on agentic and coding capabilities while the harness serves a much wider range. Case studies are honest about the mechanism - their 9B agent successfully maintains a 26-click Gomoku interaction where the Qwen3.5-9B base model's click handler throws indexing errors throughout.

Implementation notes worth stealing: the quality pipeline never lets a model judge overwrite deterministic structural facts, keeps derivation method and confidence next to every attribute, and stores evaluation coverage separately from verdicts - three cheap invariants that make later data-mixture audits trustworthy. The same structural validator doubles as the decontamination screen.

Skeptical read: this is a well-instrumented single turn of a loop, dressed in recursive-self-improvement language that the current results don't fully cash out. The routing score is a proxy for demand estimated by a router, not ground-truth difficulty, and curriculum gains could partly reflect data ordering effects rather than genuine capability feedback. That said, the prediction-action-outcome separation and the rule-first quality gates are genuinely reusable. Bottom line: a substantive agentic post-training recipe with clean data infrastructure, pitched one tier above what it's yet demonstrated. Worth watching for iteration two; don't cite it as evidence that RSI works.

For teams attempting a reproduction, the report names the stack precisely, which is a courtesy worth noting: user-turn examples are serialized with the Qwen3.5 chat template and tool-call format, reasoning is retained within the current turn but dropped from earlier turns, and serving uses SGLang version 0.5.17 with thinking mode enabled. The agentic stack spans several harnesses, including OpenSquilla, across varied real-world tasks, so the training experience spans different model behaviors and execution environments rather than one harness's quirks. The heterogeneity is presented as a feature: several generations of checkpoints from different families coexist in the pool, and the harness's records become the connective tissue between them.
Method lineage matters when sizing the delta: the trajectory-SFT strand builds on FireAct, AgentTuning, Agent-FLAN, and AgentBank, which established that data composition and scale dominate generalization; Llama 3 added synthetic multi-step tool-use data with iterative SFT, rejection sampling, and direct preference optimization. The on-policy distillation strand is Qwen's own R-OPD line and DeepSeek's practice. NeoHorse-1's particular contribution is not a new loss but the routing signal as an organizing principle for both - the same three-stage progression applied to SFT example ordering and to the starting contexts handed to the distillation student. That's a small idea executed with a large amount of pipeline discipline, and the report is at its best when describing that pipeline.
One caveat for anyone parsing the tables: benchmark contexts matter. VitaBench runs with a substituted user-simulator because the recommended one was retired, QwenClawBench and PinchBench run inside the OpenSquilla harness, and instruction-following numbers use a 51,200-token output budget. Those are disclosed, but they mean cross-paper comparisons, including our own against Agents-A1-4B and Nanbeige, should be taken as same-harness-relative rather than absolute.
